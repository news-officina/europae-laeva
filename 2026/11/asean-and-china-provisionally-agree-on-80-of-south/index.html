ASEAN and China provisionally agree on 80% of South China Sea code, leave binding open

Philippine Foreign Secretary Theresa Lazaro said only one of four major issues is settled and the time the talks need has prevented a final deal in 2026. Singapore takes over the ASEAN chairmanship with the remaining work in 2027.

16 November 2026

ASEAN and China have provisionally agreed on about 80 per cent of the South China Sea Code of Conduct text, Philippine Foreign Secretary Theresa Lazaro said. With only one of four major issues settled, she said, whether the code should be legally binding is still open.

ASEAN itself is not united. The Philippines wants the code to have stronger binding force, while other members differ on scope of application and limits on military activities, Thepaper reported. Lazaro said some countries see binding force as something that could require ratification by each member state. The settled issue is how the new code relates to the Declaration on the Conduct of Parties, its foundation.

The code is meant to set rules for managing tensions in the disputed South China Sea, where Beijing's expansive maritime claims overlap with those of several Southeast Asian nations, Channelnewsasia reported. Lazaro said the time the talks need had prevented a final deal in 2026. Under the Philippine chairmanship, negotiators held nine rounds of talks and two senior officials' meetings this year. The discussions took place monthly.

Singapore takes over the rotating ASEAN chairmanship in 2027, and the remaining work on the code passes with it. "I trust that Singapore will be able to finish the discussions," she said. China holds that the so-called arbitration award is unlawful and void, and that it has nothing to do with the code.

Washington has already laid down its condition. US Ambassador to Asean Kevin Kim said any outcome must not undermine the interests of the United States. Former Supreme Court Associate Justice Antonio Carpio said nothing will happen with the code this year or next year.
