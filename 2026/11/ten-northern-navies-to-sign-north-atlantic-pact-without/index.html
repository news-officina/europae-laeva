Ten northern navies to sign North Atlantic pact without the US

The ten Joint Expeditionary Force countries, put forward by Norway, will share doctrine, training, equipment and ammunition under the Royal Navy's Maritime Proposition, after Washington told allies in July it would cut its NATO fighter aircraft by a third.

9 November 2026

Ten naval forces will sign a partnership agreement on 10 November to deter future threats from Russia, the countries of the Joint Expeditionary Force, a ten-country naval cooperation grouping, said. They are Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom. Under the Maritime Proposition, set out by the Royal Navy in July, they will align doctrine and training and swap equipment and ammunition across fleets.

The timing matters. In July, Washington told European allies it would cut the fighter aircraft it makes available to NATO in a crisis by a third, the Spanish newspaper El País reported. It would halve its strategic bombers, provide no submarines and withdraw 5,000 troops from Germany. Norway's Defence Minister Tore O. Sandvik said Europe and Canada are taking greater responsibility for maritime security. He said the result will be a stronger Europe and a stronger NATO.

The Maritime Proposition was formally agreed by all ten Joint Expeditionary Force nations on 9 July, the Royal Navy said. The grouping complements NATO rather than replaces it, Britain's First Sea Lord, General Gwyn Jenkins, told Russian outlets reporting on the plan. Joint forces, he said, would be commanded from Northwood, the Royal Navy's main maritime headquarters in London. First Sea Lord General Sir Gwyn Jenkins said they could respond to crises faster than under existing arrangements.

Norway put the partnership forward. Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre said the initiative by Europe and Canada makes a significant contribution to enhancing security in the North Atlantic and High North, and is a direct response to calls by the United States for other countries to assume more responsibility for the security of Allied territory.

The United States will not join the joint naval forces, though Canada's participation is still under consideration. Russia, for its part, denounced the grouping. Andrei Denisov, deputy chair of the foreign affairs committee of the Federation Council, Russia's upper house of parliament, called it another provocation and escalation, saying Britain no longer hides behind small European states.
