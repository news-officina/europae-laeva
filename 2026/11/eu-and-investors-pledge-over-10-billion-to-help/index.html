EU and investors pledge over €10 billion to help European tech firms grow global

The EIB Group, 15 institutional investors and seven member states have pledged over €10 billion and hope to mobilise up to €80 billion in total, from a first closing of around €2 billion. The money is a pledge, not cash, and the European Commission calculated EU venture funding was about 84 percent below the American level in 2024.

10 November 2026, updated 11 November 2026

The EIB Group, 15 institutional investors and seven EU member states have pledged over €10 billion in new equity for European scale-ups. The alliance aims to mobilise up to €80 billion in total, with average tickets of €200 million, to help European tech companies grow global.

The first closing of the Tech Champions fund-of-funds stands at around €2 billion in commitments. The EIB Group agreed it with Bulgaria, Germany, Greece, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Italy and Spain, paving the way for the first investment approvals. Alongside it, the European Commission committed €100 million to the EIB Group's Defence Equity Facility 2.0. It invests in defence, space and cybersecurity.

On the private side, the French Tibi Initiative and 14 financial institutions, among them ABN AMRO, APG, Banco Santander, BBVA, Danske Bank Group and PGGM, have declared the intention to channel more than €8 billion combined. The European Commission and the EIB Group also jointly launched the European Institutional Investors Pact. It is meant to make it simpler for institutional investors, such as pension funds and insurers, to put money to work. The funds flow through the EIB Group-led Investment Platform.

In July 2026, all EU27 governments unanimously backed the expansion of that fund of funds, ETCI 2.0, launched together with private institutional investors. The fund of funds has a fundraising target of up to €15 billion, around four times the size of the original launched in 2023. The European Commission calculated that in 2024 around $133 billion of venture capital flowed into later-stage growth companies in the USA, compared with $21.3 billion in the EU. At the ECB euro reference rate, that is about €119 billion compared with about €19 billion. That is about 84 percent below the American level.

What remains open is timing and conversion. The €8 billion from private institutions is not cash, and the €80 billion goal is an ambition rather than a plan. According to Europreneurs Media, the test comes within weeks, when the first investment decisions show how fast the money reaches the companies.
