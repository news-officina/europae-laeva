Meta, Google and TikTok sue to block California ban on addictive feeds for under-16s

The companies filed suit on November 4 against Assembly Bill 1709, which they call a violation of free speech. Attorney General Rob Bonta's office says it will defend the law in court.

7 November 2026

Meta, Google and TikTok are asking a federal court to strike down California's addictive-feed ban for under-16s before January 1, 2027. The companies filed separate lawsuits on November 4 against Assembly Bill 1709, a California law, saying the restriction of these features violates the First Amendment, which protects free speech in the United States. They also want California Attorney General Rob Bonta barred from enforcing it.

In August, US District Judge Edward Davila refused the social media companies' request for a preliminary injunction, a court order halting a law, against Senate Bill 976, an earlier California law on social media design. He also rejected their bid to keep the law on hold while they sought review by the Ninth Circuit, the federal appeals court for the western United States. EPIC, a digital rights group, says the new lawsuits mostly rehash arguments that the court already rejected in August.

Meta says it is challenging the law because it would force the company to either remove age-appropriate features or cut thousands of California teens off from their communities. TikTok called Assembly Bill 1709 a blatantly unconstitutional restriction on free expression that takes away parents' rights to decide what is appropriate for their kids. Google said parents are best positioned to make decisions for their families, pointing to the parental controls and tools it already offers.

Youth advocates say the companies are suing because the law works. Nikki Iyer, co-chair of Design It For Us, a youth-led coalition, said the companies know it works and it should be enforced. "Google, Meta, and TikTok are suing to block AB 1709 because they know it works," she said. She also said Big Tech and its front groups keep running to court to stop California from regulating harmful design. Courts, she said, increasingly side with California.

Bonta's office says it is reviewing the lawsuits and will defend the law. A spokesperson said California's commonsense laws protect kids online. Governor Gavin Newsom signed Assembly Bill 1709 on September 10.
