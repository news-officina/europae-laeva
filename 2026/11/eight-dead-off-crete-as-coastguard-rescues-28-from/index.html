At least 20 dead in two boats off Crete, two still missing

The Greek coastguard arrested two suspected smugglers after a dinghy was found in distress south of Kali Limenes. Deputy minister Sevi Voloudaki says Minister Pleuris will announce within days a plan for return hubs outside Europe's territory.

9 November 2026

Correction: An earlier version left out a second boat, found off Crete early on Monday 9 November with 12 dead and 18 survivors, and placed Monday's details with the boat found on Saturday.

The Greek coastguard recovered the bodies of 12 people and rescued 18 from a dinghy off southern Crete in the early hours of Monday, after a Moroccan-flagged container ship spotted it 15 nautical miles south-east of Kali Limenes. On Saturday evening the coastguard had recovered eight bodies and rescued 28 refugees and migrants from another boat, with two people still missing. Sevi Voloudaki, the deputy minister of migration and asylum, said Minister Pleuris will announce within days a plan, in cooperation with other EU member states, for so-called return hubs in a country outside Europe's territory.

The cooperation that once existed with eastern Libya is gone in recent days, and the flows have grown as a result. The survivors themselves put a price on the journey. They paid between 12,000 and 16,000 Libyan dinars each for the crossing, a claim reported by ERT, Iefimerida and Ta Nea but disputed. They said they left the Libyan coast on 3 or 4 November.

The coastguard was first told of the Saturday boat on Friday afternoon, when a dinghy carrying foreigners was reported in distress six nautical miles south-southwest of Kali Limenes. A local coastguard official, speaking to Reuters on condition of anonymity, said: "We found them shivering from the cold".

The Heraklion Central Coast Guard Authority is leading the preliminary investigation. It arrested two Sudanese nationals, aged 28 and 16, after survivors identified them as the smugglers. The authority then opened a case file against the two. The charges are illegal entry into the country, facilitating illegal entry, forming a gang and fatal exposure, according to reporting by ERT, Iefimerida and Ta Nea. Brussels Times, Euronews and Ta Nea corroborated the arrests. One of four survivors of the Monday dinghy who were taken to hospital later died, Al Jazeera and The National reported.

Voloudaki said efforts continue at diplomatic and bilateral level, in cooperation with the European Union and the European Commission, to find a solution. She spoke of the dead as people who are victims of smuggling networks, referring to the migrants found dead on Crete.
