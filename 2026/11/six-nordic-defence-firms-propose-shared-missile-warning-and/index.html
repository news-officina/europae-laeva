Six Nordic defence firms propose shared missile warning and Arctic surveillance

The Nordic Compass statement, led by former Finnish Prime Minister Jyrki Katainen, also sets out four steps towards one Nordic defence market. Governments of the five Nordic countries are invited to join.

6 November 2026

Six Nordic defence companies have proposed a shared space-based missile warning system, stronger Arctic surveillance and a secure communications network. They set this out in their joint Nordic Compass Defence Statement, published in Gothenburg on 5 November 2026. The statement also proposes four first steps towards one Nordic defence market. They include mutual recognition of certification and a pilot framework contract that pays for production capacity.

Patria's President and CEO, Panu Routila, said the Nordic forces need capabilities faster and that closer cooperation must deliver them. He said the countries still run five separate procurement chains despite record spending. The companies argue budgets alone are not enough, and that a shared market makes the region safer and drives the economy. They invite the governments of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, in particular their defence authorities and defence forces, to join in.

The statement comes from a wider grouping. Nordic Compass, a Round Table for industry led by Finland's former Prime Minister Jyrki Katainen, was launched on 12 May 2026. It gathers 30 leading Nordic companies, foundations and organisations across four tracks. Ericsson, Kongsberg, Nokia, Patria and Saab set it up in August 2026 to strengthen security in Europe, and the Danish defence company Terma later joined them.

Kongsberg chief executive Eirik Lie, Terma chief executive Henriette Hallberg Thygesen and Patria chief executive Panu Routila attended in person.

Finland's President Alexander Stubb, the country's head of state, was to give the opening speech, according to the Finnish President's Office. Sweden's Defence Minister Pål Jonson was also to take part, the Swedish Ministry of Defence said in a press release.
