Quantum Systems drone fleet flying again after cyberattack on update server

The Bundeswehr had grounded a large share of the fleet as a precaution, the Financial Times reported, and federal authorities are investigating the attack that Der Spiegel reported reached Bundeswehr servers before anyone detected it.

13 November 2026

Quantum Systems' drones are flying again with the German armed forces. The Financial Times reports that the Bundeswehr, Germany's armed forces, grounded a large share of the fleet as a precaution after a cyberattack, which also kept the company out of a recent military exercise. The fleet, the company said, is operating normally worldwide, including in Germany. Germany's defence ministry will not say anything about the readiness of the equipment.

Unknown attackers got a malicious program onto an internal update server of Quantum Systems, a supplier to the Bundeswehr. Several German federal authorities are investigating. The company says the attack is part of a longer pattern it has described. The European defence industry, it said, has been facing persistent cyberattacks for years. Companies at the forefront of supporting Ukraine are targeted precisely because their work is critically relevant to customers' security, the company said.

The Qbase software plans the flights of Vector reconnaissance drones supplied to Ukraine's Defence Forces and to the Bundeswehr, where they are known as the Falke, or falcon. The Financial Times reported that Quantum Systems has more than 200 staff in Ukraine and three joint ventures with Ukrainian firms that build small attack drones and ground vehicles. It is not clear whether those operations were affected.

According to Der Spiegel, the malicious update never reached the drones themselves, but it had spread to Bundeswehr servers and those of other customers, where an inspection uncovered it. Quantum Systems' own investigation found an active cyberattack by hostile actors. The company said its IT and security teams contained the threat with a swift response, working closely and transparently with the authorities. Der Spiegel reported that the program reached the systems before anyone detected it.

Quantum Systems announced a $1.2 billion funding round in July 2026, valuing the company at around $8 billion. That same month, the BfV, Germany's domestic intelligence agency, warned that Germany's defense sector, and companies supporting Ukraine in particular, faced a higher risk of Russian cyber attacks, espionage and sabotage.
