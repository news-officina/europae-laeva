Italy fines Meta €805 million over Facebook feature that read phone photo galleries

The privacy watchdog found 20 million Italian Android users never gave specific consent. Meta says the fine is disproportionate and has 60 days to ask users again.

14 November 2026

Italy's data-protection authority fined Meta Platforms Ireland €805 million on 14 November over a Facebook feature, available since 2023, called "Get tips from your gallery while browsing Facebook" that suggested photos and videos from Android phones' galleries as material to post. About 20 million Android users in Italy were affected.

The authority found that gallery access came with a single permission valid for the whole app, and that the feature was active by default. Its generic notice to "enable other features" did not tell users they were also agreeing to gallery suggestions. Such a permission, granted in full, lets the app read a device's entire photo archive, usually permanently. The fine is about 0.47% of Meta's global revenue. In the third quarter the company reported about €58 billion ($64.60 billion) in revenue, up 26%, almost all of it from advertising.

The case began in July 2025, when the Bolzano Consumer Protection Center reported the feature to the Garante, which then ran its own technical analysis. Through the Facebook app for Android devices, Meta read photos and videos from users' phones, with their metadata, without specific consent, the authority found. Its legal basis is article 122 of the Italian privacy code, which applies the European ePrivacy directive. Under that rule, the smartphone counts as an extension of a person's private sphere. Consent must be free and informed, whether or not data is sent anywhere or harm follows.

The Garante weighed the violation's duration, which continues for users who have not updated the app, and a previous 2019 ruling against Meta. It cited what it called Meta's lack of co-operation and the high number of people affected. Meta told the BBC it strongly disagreed with the regulator's assessment of the facts, its application of the law, and the disproportionate level of the fine. The company said users must grant permission on their device before the feature can operate, and can change it in their settings. The authority also rejected Meta's objection that Ireland's regulator had jurisdiction, because ePrivacy rules do not provide for the 'one-stop-shop' mechanism.

The Garante has given Meta 60 days to ask for consent again, this time with a separate and specific request. The request must explain what the feature does and how to switch it off. The feature must stay off by default, the authority says, including for users who already granted consent. Meta must also report on its first steps within 30 days and set out all its measures within 90 days.
