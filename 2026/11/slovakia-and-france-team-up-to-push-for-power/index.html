Slovakia and France team up to push for power bills decoupled from gas prices

President Peter Pellegrini called the EU's gas-linked pricing illogical for a country that does not depend on gas for its electricity. The visit also brought a 120 million euro helicopter deal and talks on defence cooperation.

13 November 2026

Slovakia and France will jointly push for a reform of European electricity pricing so that power bills no longer follow the price of gas. Slovak President Peter Pellegrini called it illogical that electricity prices are tied to the gas price when Slovakia is not dependent on gas for its electricity production.

The point matters most for countries that produce electricity from nuclear plants. Pellegrini said the EU's price setting does not reflect the advantages of nuclear energy. Slovakia cannot turn that advantage into lower bills for its industry and households, or into stronger competitiveness, he said.

The talks also produced a signed contract: three heavy helicopters for Slovak police and rescuers, built by Airbus in France, worth 120 million euros. France is to bid in a tender for a possible delivery of additional lighter helicopters for the same forces.

Technical negotiations between the Slovak and French defence ministries are under way on deeper cooperation in the defence industry. France, he said, is Slovakia's eighth most important trading market, and French companies employ more than 35,000 people there. He also said Europe cannot be left out of talks on Ukraine and the continent's future security arrangement. Pellegrini called for a diplomatic settlement as soon as possible. In his words, a military solution would bring no desired result and would cause more victims and damage.

Slovakia's nuclear plans still leave room for France. Pellegrini said the reactor for Slovakia's planned new nuclear source will come from the American company Westinghouse. French firms can still supply components, nuclear fuel and other technologies, he said, and France could also cooperate on reusing spent nuclear fuel.
