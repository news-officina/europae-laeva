Maileva breach exposed personal data across 39 million mail references

The breach hit clients in social housing, health and public services, Cyberattaque.org reported, and by 9 November neither Maileva nor La Poste had published a statement, according to the site Ostraca. The CNIL was notified the same evening the platform was shut down.

14 November 2026

A breach at Maileva exposed the personal data of senders and recipients, including names, postal and email addresses, across 39 million mail references. The occupational health service in Cornouaille confirmed the access on 9 October. Neither Maileva nor La Poste, the French postal group, had published a statement about the leak by 9 November, according to the census kept by the site Ostraca.

Several thousand client companies are also affected, notably in the social housing, health and public services sectors, Cyberattaque.org reported. FrenchBreaches reports the exposed recipient data as gender, first and last name and postal address. The sender data includes account name, login identifier, email address and the name of the file attached to the mail.

The organisations whose mail went through Maileva have begun telling the people concerned. On 2 November, the Inovie laboratory group published a notice saying patients of several of its laboratories were affected by the leak at its mail-sending provider. The next day, Flandre Opale Habitat, a social landlord with about 20,000 homes across 259 communes, warned its tenants of an incident at Maileva. It said mail sent over the last ten years may have exposed identities, contact details, client numbers, housing information and administrative or financial elements such as invoice amounts.

Maileva has told its clients that its investigations confirmed attempts of unauthorized access by a cybercriminal seeking to carry out illegitimate mailings. It notified the CNIL, the French data protection authority, at 6pm on 23 September, the day it shut the platform down.

Maileva, a brand of Docaposte, the digital subsidiary of the La Poste group, shut the whole platform down from 23 September at 7 a.m. Maileva described the move as "proactive", and all clients were affected. Docaposte's press service said on 29 September that the services were once again fully operational that day.
