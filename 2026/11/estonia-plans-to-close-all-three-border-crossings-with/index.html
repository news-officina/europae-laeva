Estonia plans to close all three border crossings with Russia from 1 December

The Interior Minister, Igor Taro, will propose closing the crossings at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula after a thwarted sabotage attempt on defence firm Threod Systems that Estonia blames on Russia's GRU.

16 November 2026

Estonia's Government plans to close all three road border crossings with Russia, at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula, to pedestrians and vehicles from 1 December 2026. It acted after a sabotage attempt on defence company Threod Systems that Estonia says was commissioned by Russia's military intelligence agency, the GRU. Residents and cargo drivers have about two weeks to return. Estonian Interior Minister Igor Taro said he will propose the closure to the Government.

It is not yet known whether the closure of the Estonian-Russian border will be temporary or indefinite. Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna has been in contact with his counterparts in Lithuania, Latvia and Finland regarding the closure, according to the foreign ministry. He also informed colleagues from the EU about it.

In the early hours of a Saturday, an attempt was made to attack the Threod Systems building with the aim of causing an explosion and setting it on fire. No one was injured, and the company's production and deliveries were unaffected. On the night of 14 November 2026, eight Latvian nationals were detained on Estonian territory, and the attempted sabotage was thwarted as a result of cooperation between the Latvian State Security Service (VDD) and the Estonian Internal Security Service (KAPO).

Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said closing the border would reduce the threat to people in Estonia. He said it would send a clear message that attacks on Estonia's defence industry and defence capabilities are unacceptable. The Prosecutor's Office has applied to the court to have five of the suspects in the Threod Systems attack remanded in custody.

Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said the closure would reduce Russia's ability to conduct hostile activities against Estonia and help mitigate risks of sanctions circumvention. Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said Moscow will verify reports that Tallinn intends to close its border with Russia. She said that if the plans to close the border are Estonia's real plans, they fit into another outburst of aggressive and unbridled Russophobia. Andrii Sybiha wrote on X that Estonia proved Russian sabotage can and should have consequences beyond simple diplomatic condemnation, and that such an approach deserves broader support across Europe.
