Republican Massie files eight impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Hegseth

The Kentucky congressman accuses Hegseth of waging war on Iran without Congress's approval and of unlawful strikes he says killed hundreds of civilians. House Speaker Mike Johnson calls the move a political trick.

9 November 2026

Representative Thomas Massie, a Republican from Kentucky, has introduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over the war on Iran. The first article accuses Hegseth of directing armed hostilities against Iran without a declaration of war or authorization from Congress. Massie alleges Hegseth abused his office and committed high crimes and misdemeanors.

Massie says Hegseth directed military operations that killed or injured more than 200 civilians at an elementary school in Minab, Iran. According to Massie, Hegseth also directed unlawful lethal force, resulting in the summary execution of at least 243 people in international waters, under false legal authorities. Massie's campaign began in September 2026. House Speaker Mike Johnson then canceled the final voting day before the midterm recess, adjourning the House rather than allow a vote.

Massie says Hegseth is violating three specific provisions of the War Powers Resolution of 1973. A further article accuses Hegseth of authorizing force inconsistent with the law of armed conflict, citing illegal double tap strikes in the southern Caribbean.

In a video on social media on Thursday, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth dismissed the impeachment effort as a politically motivated campaign by a "disgruntled, defeated congressman". He added: "We don't back down and that especially applies now." Brad Cooper, head of U.S. Central Command, pushed in recent briefings for a return to major combat operations against Iran. Vice President JD Vance and special envoy Steve Witkoff pressed for diplomacy. Vance also blamed the midterm loss on the war, writing on X: "The reason we lost is the war."

Speaker Mike Johnson condemned Massie's resolution on Monday as a "cheap trick" for political gain. He said impeaching a Cabinet secretary amid hostilities abroad would be a terrible thing for allies and enemies to see. But two Republican members told MS NOW they believe a vote could succeed. "I think the numbers are there," one said, though he prefers Hegseth resign rather than Congress act.
