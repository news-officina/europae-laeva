Italy to give Zelensky's troops access to its radar satellites in thirteenth aid package

Defence Minister Guido Crosetto will present the package to the security committee Copasir on 10 November and propose extending arms supplies into 2027, as Salvini proposes halting arms and money for Ukraine.

10 November 2026

Italy's Defence Minister Guido Crosetto will present Italy's thirteenth military aid package for Ukraine to Copasir, the parliamentary committee that oversees security and intelligence, on 10 November. The package includes access for Volodymyr Zelensky's troops to images from two Cosmo-SkyMed radar satellites. Crosetto will also propose extending the decree-based supply mechanism for Kyiv into 2027.

What Italy will actually send is not public: the decree, also signed by Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani and Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti, is secret, as earlier packages were. The Institute of International Affairs estimates the total value of Italian military aid has passed 3 billion euros. In September, a poll conducted for the Ixè institute found 18 percent of Italians backed continuing arms supplies.

The governing coalition is openly split. Matteo Salvini, newly re-elected as Lega leader at the party's congress in Milan, said he would propose a halt to arms and money for Ukraine. He would raise it in the Council of Ministers. Tajani answered that Italy stands with Ukraine and will help it for as long as necessary. "The next military aid package is already ready, and we will send it to Kyiv as soon as possible," Tajani said.

Crosetto has defended the mechanism before. The decrees were chosen and voted for by the Draghi government and practically all parties now in opposition and in the majority, he said. "If it was right then, it is hard to explain why it is unjust now." Under the decrees, he added, Italy transfers only equipment to Ukraine, with no direct money changing hands. Together with Tajani, he has framed the assistance as part of Italy's commitments to the European Union and NATO.

The current decree allowing arms supplies to Ukraine expires on 31 December, and Italy approved the previous package almost a year ago. Use of the satellite data will be limited to Ukrainian territory, so it cannot serve attacks on Russia.
