Belgrade judge detains special police commander on torture suspicion

The head of Serbia's SAJ unit faces up to 30 days in detention over the alleged torture of ten people in a government garage. The Belgrade Bar Association says the timing of arrests at the close of the election campaign raises suspicion of political use of police and prosecutors.

7 November 2026

A judge at the First Basic Court in Belgrade has ordered Igor Žmirić, commander of the SAJ special police unit, into detention for up to 30 days on suspicion of torture. Vladimir Prijović, president of the Belgrade Bar Association, said the timing of the arrests and searches at the close of the campaign raises suspicion. He said police and prosecutors are being used for political ends before the 25 October parliamentary elections.

The detention order rests on the suspicion that Žmirić and other suspects, in co-perpetration with first defendant M. K., inflicted great pain and severe suffering on ten named persons in the Government garage on the night of 14-15 August 2025. Candidates of the Student List demanded an investigation into whether chief prosecutor Nenad Stefanović used his powers to favor the defendants.

The interior ministry (MUP) said the suspects acted as JZO officials and used force and threats in the garage of the Government building on Nemanjina Street. According to the MUP, they did this to obtain information, intimidate and unlawfully punish the victims. The First Basic Court said detention was ordered so the suspects would not influence witnesses. It said they allegedly committed a violent crime punishable by more than five years in a way that caused public anxiety.

The closing days of the campaign also brought arrests and searches. On the order of the Higher Public Prosecutor's Office in Belgrade, police arrested Srđan Ilić from Zemun on suspicion of organising and training several people to provoke violence during and after the 25 October 2026 parliamentary elections. In a coordinated action, five people were arrested in Novi Sad on suspicion that on 25 October 2026 they planned violence and disorder aimed at endangering Serbia's constitutional order and security. UKP officers also searched the flat of Andrej Premović, a RAF student suspected of unauthorised access to a protected computer. The prosecution said he was not arrested and was not placed in detention.

Prijović warned that the coincidence in timing between the police actions and the close of the election campaign is not a circumstance the country can afford to ignore. He said it is especially worrying if such actions impose an image of students as a threat to public order and the constitutional system.
