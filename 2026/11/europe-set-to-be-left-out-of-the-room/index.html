Europe set to be left out of the room as Russia and Ukraine meet in Abu Dhabi

Kaja Kallas warns the EU will not implement any deal it is not part of, and the row over representation now goes on the agenda when EU leaders meet on 13 November.

9 November 2026

On 9 November, Grigory Karasin, first deputy chairman of the international affairs committee of the Federation Council, the Russian upper house of parliament, said no agreement exists for European states, including France, to join the talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, and called Paris's plans wishful thinking. Jean-Noel Barrot said France will be in Abu Dhabi, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said, "The EU will be present alongside our partners". Moscow keeps rejecting an unconditional ceasefire and demands Ukraine cede Donbas.

An EU official said, according to Politico, that Europe's representatives would not be inside the room for the Abu Dhabi talks. Kaja Kallas, the EU's foreign policy chief, replied that key parts of any settlement depend on decisions by European institutions. She named sanctions, frozen Russian assets, reconstruction and Ukraine's path to EU membership. "Any agreement that we are not party of, we are not going to implement either," she said.

The Kremlin gave its own account. Dmitry Peskov, President Vladimir Putin's press secretary, said the talks in Abu Dhabi in the coming days would be trilateral, between Russia and Ukraine with the United States as mediator. In his account, no Europeans would take part. Barrot said the same in an interview on 8 November. Von der Leyen said the same on Monday after a call with Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

The dispute over who sits at the table runs through EU capitals themselves. According to Politico, leaders have been at loggerheads over how Europe should be represented at the talks. Radosław Sikorski, Poland's foreign minister, said a deal made over Europe's head would be impossible. He called for a ceasefire before any talks on European security arrangements. Zelensky said on 25 October that Ukraine's guarantees are its own and cannot be dictated by the Russian side.

The row over who speaks for Europe now has a place on the agenda. Giorgia Meloni, Italy's prime minister, voiced frustration at the number of meetings and the plurality of representatives. She made sure the subject goes on the agenda when EU leaders meet in Ireland on 13 November. Across the Atlantic, Donald Trump welcomed the talks, predicting the war would end soon. He said the war will end only because of him.
