Iraq proposes ministerial shuttle between Tehran and Washington as losses pass $60bn

Oil revenues lost since February 2026 equal about €54 billion, and oil funds roughly 90 percent of the federal budget. Foreign minister Fuad Hussein's plan follows Iranian terms for resuming talks, including reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

7 November 2026

Foreign minister Fuad Hussein has proposed a committee of regional foreign ministers to travel between Tehran and Washington and seek a peaceful settlement. His plan comes as Iraq pays a heavy price for the US war on Iran: since that war began in February 2026, the country has lost more than $60 billion in oil revenues. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz, through which about 90 percent of its oil exports normally pass.

The loss weighs heavier because oil provides about 90 percent of the federal budget. US President Donald Trump launched the war on Iran. The $60 billion shortfall equals about €54 billion at the European Central Bank's euro reference rate of 6 November.

On 6 November, Hussein received a phone call from Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Araghchi set out Iran's terms for resuming talks with Washington: timelines for negotiation, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, returning to the understandings reached in Pakistan, and further points on the nuclear file. The Iranian proposal had been laid out before the US midterm elections. Hussein answered with a warning: tensions in the region are rising, renewed military operations remain possible, and practical steps are needed to contain the escalation.

Baghdad also speaks directly with Washington. On 5 November, Iraqi Prime Minister Ali Falih al-Zaidi held a phone call with US President Donald Trump. They discussed regional developments, security and prospects for the next phase. In the call, al-Zaidi reaffirmed respect for national sovereignty and that Iraqi territory must not be a launchpad for attacks on neighbouring countries. Hussein, who has told Dijlah TV that renewed US attacks on Iran are 'not a secret', has warned Iraqi armed groups against joining any Iranian retaliation.

In late October, Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair said exports had recovered to over 3.4 million barrels per day, nearing the pre-war level of 3.7 million. Under the 90-day de-escalation deal al-Zaidi opened, the Iran-aligned factions halt attacks and receive guarantees that US forces will not strike them. This is the first stage of a plan to bring their weapons under state control by June 30, 2027.
