Japan weighs 50 to 60 trillion yen for defence from fiscal 2027

Finance and defence ministries have begun coordinating the five-year sum, Nikkei reports, well below the Defense Ministry's own estimate of about 80 trillion yen. An income tax rise to help pay for it, decided in December 2025, starts in January 2027.

13 November 2026, updated 14 November 2026

Correction: An earlier version led on the income tax decision of December 2025 as if it were new, and gave dates without their year.

Japan's finance and defence ministries have begun coordinating defence spending for the five years from fiscal 2027. A range of 50 to 60 trillion yen is in sight for strengthening the country's defence capability, Nikkei reported.

Part of the money is to come from an income tax rise. On 18 December 2025, Japan's government and ruling coalition decided to raise income tax from January 2027 to fund defence capability building, Nikkei reported at the time. At the same time, it would cut the Reconstruction Special Income Tax by 1 point, so that on a single-year basis the tax burden would not increase for the time being. The day before, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi had said the government aimed for a settlement on the defence tax that would not bring a new increase in household burdens.

The current plan has its roots at the end of fiscal 2022. It raised scheduled spending for the five years through fiscal 2026 from 27.5 trillion yen to 43 trillion yen. That money is going mainly to long-range missiles meant as counterattack capability, equipment parts and ammunition. Japan's Ministry of Defense has put its requested spending at about 80 trillion yen for the five years that follow, a sum Sankei reported as the ministry's own estimate.

The security-related documents due for revision within the year would set total defence spending at around 60 trillion yen over those same five years, with coordination still under way, Yahoo reported. At the same time, the United States is asking allies to raise defence spending to 3.5 percent of GDP.

The revised security documents, reports say, would keep describing China as Japan's greatest strategic challenge and commit to more defence spending over the next five years. China's Ministry of National Defense spokesperson, Senior Colonel Chen Xi, said Japan's right-wing forces keep fabricating the so-called China threat, portraying Japan as a victim, and expanding offensive and long-range strike capabilities.
