Opinion

An asteroid shield is common defence. Fund it from the Union.

Europe paid for Hera and it works, so the defence itself should be a public mission under the common budget, financed by common borrowing repaid from a tax on the largest fortunes.

18 November 2026

An American strike deflected an asteroid, and a European camera now watches it. On 25 November in Berlin, ESA will unveil the first detailed images of Dimorphos since the impact. NASA's 580-kg craft had hit the asteroid in 2022 and shortened its orbit by 33 minutes. Member states treat planetary defence as a space-science subscription, an optional extra they renew with pride. It is common defence, and the Commission should bring it inside the Union's common defence as a public mission under the common budget.

What the public paid for has already delivered. ESA reported on 11 November that Hera had reached the Didymos system; from 25 to 30 kilometres away it has begun its early characterisation phase. Its Asteroid Framing Camera took the first close-up from 60 kilometres, with most of the 151-metre Dimorphos hidden in shadow beside the 780-metre Didymos. Engineers in Darmstadt fly it at 12 cm/s around the pair, nearly 200 million km from Earth. What Europe lacks is a decision to own the defence itself.

ESA says the unknowns stand in the way: whether the strike left a crater or reshaped Dimorphos, and its mass, structure and makeup. A new simulation study led from the University of Bern suggests no crater remains. In the team's model, the whole weak rubble pile was remodelled: about 1% of Dimorphos's mass was thrown into space and 8% shifted around the body. Hera project scientist Michael Küppers warned that craft 'a bit heavier or faster than DART may have destroyed the asteroid, which is not a desirable outcome for planetary defence.' A defence that rests on open questions is not yet a capability Europe holds.

The case for the old arrangement runs: the shield works, so Europe should watch, learn and pay for the inspection. Those people say NASA settled the question in 2022 and one follow-up is enough. But watching an American hammer is not owning a defence, and Küppers's warning shows how little room for error a deflection leaves.

The bill is this. When the Commission sets out the next multiannual financial framework, it should propose survey, deflector and the follow-up chain Hera began as a public mission under common defence, financed by common borrowing repaid from a European tax on the largest fortunes. The mission is public, so its data and instruments stay in public hands. No shareholder books the profit from a shield. That is the bill, and the Commission holds the pen.
