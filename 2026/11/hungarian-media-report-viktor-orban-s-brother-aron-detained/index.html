Hungarian media report Viktor Orbán's brother Áron detained in visa fraud raids

Four people were held in Budapest, Fót and Dunakeszi over a scheme that charged Vietnamese citizens recruitment fees for visas to reach Western Europe. Viktor Orbán called the investigation a witch-hunt against him.

10 November 2026

Four individuals were apprehended on Monday morning in Budapest, Fót and Dunakeszi as part of an investigation into people smuggling and corruption. Local media and political figures said the raids resulted in the detention of Áron Orbán, the younger brother of former Prime Minister Viktor Orbán. Investigators suspect a scheme that charged Vietnamese citizens a recruitment fee for visas used to reach Western Europe.

Telex reported, citing sources close to Áron Orbán, that the inquiry was not sped up before the elections to shield a well-connected player who was feuding with him. In September, the asset recovery office said a group tightly linked to leading political figures was suspected of guaranteeing, for payment, that applicants would receive the necessary visas.

The National Investigation Agency suspects the three men and the woman of people-smuggling committed as part of a criminal conspiracy for financial gain. The asset recovery office has charged one of them with influence peddling as an instigator, and plans measures restricting the personal liberty of all four. On Monday morning, agents from the Counterterrorism Center appeared at a property in Fótliget linked to a company owned by Áron Orbán, Telex reported. The suspects were escorted in three riot police minibuses to a detention facility, where on Monday evening a photographer for the Hungarian news site HVG photographed Áron Orbán in a police van.

Viktor Orbán answered in a social media post that the witch-hunt over his brother is a tool for diverting attention, and that what is happening is reckoning. He wrote that the goal of the dirty game is to eliminate political opponents and set up a one-party system. He said he himself is the target, while his brother is merely a means to an end. Péter Magyar, the opposition leader, replied on Facebook that the former prime minister, as usual, immediately pointed at others and posed as a victim. "As you sow, so shall you reap," he wrote.

In September, Telex reported that after the fall of Fidesz, two of Áron Orbán's partners involved in the case were arrested, while the prime minister's brother remained untouched. The scheme rested on a contract first obtained by Népszava. In early 2024, Multi Shoot, a company led by Orbán as chief executive, paid the University of Pécs €30,000 per group to train Vietnamese visitors. The fee covered groups of 20 over two days, excluding tax. The university confirmed the contract as genuine and said two Vietnamese groups completed the training.
